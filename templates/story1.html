A Routine Admission That Changed Everything

Mrs. Anita Sharma, 74, was admitted to City General Hospital's Geriatric Ward in early March 2024 for post-operative recovery following a successful hip replacement surgery. While the surgery itself had gone smoothly, the recovery period carried significant risk — elderly patients recovering from orthopaedic procedures face a heightened chance of secondary falls, which can undo surgical outcomes and dramatically extend hospital stays.

Her family, particularly her daughter Meera, was deeply anxious. "My mother is fiercely independent," Meera told our patient liaison team. "She would never call for help until it was too late. That was our biggest fear."

The Night FallGuard Intervened

On the sixth night of her admission, at 2:17 AM, Mrs. Sharma attempted to reach her water bottle from the bedside table — a motion she had made hundreds of times throughout her life. But post-operative stiffness and mild disorientation from her pain medication made the movement dangerous. FallGuard's CNN+LSTM model, processing her skeletal data in real time, detected an abnormal torso angle and rapidly accelerating hip velocity inconsistent with intentional reaching.

Within 2.1 seconds of the movement anomaly, the system's confidence score crossed the alert threshold for 8 consecutive detection windows. A silent alarm was sent simultaneously to the ward nursing station and the duty nurse's mobile terminal.

Ward Nurse Kavitha arrived at Room 101 within 47 seconds. She found Mrs. Sharma mid-way through a forward lean that, without intervention, would have resulted in a floor-level fall — likely causing a fracture to her recently repaired hip.

Nurse on Duty — Kavitha R.

"The alert was specific and immediate. It told me exactly which room, and I could see on the dashboard that the confidence was at 93.4%. I didn't have to guess — I just went. That's what made all the difference."

Recovery Uninterrupted

Because the intervention happened before a fall occurred, Mrs. Sharma's recovery timeline was completely unaffected. She was discharged ten days after admission, on the originally planned date, with full mobility targets met. Her orthopaedic consultant noted that a secondary fall at that stage of recovery would have required corrective surgery and an additional four to six weeks of inpatient rehabilitation.

For Mrs. Sharma, the experience left a lasting impression. "I didn't even know anyone was watching," she said during her discharge interview. "Not in an uncomfortable way — I never felt observed. I just felt safe. That's a rare thing when you're in hospital at 2 in the morning."

What Privacy-First Monitoring Meant for the Sharma Family

One of the family's initial concerns, raised during the admission process, was about camera-based monitoring. Meera had reservations about her mother being filmed overnight. The nursing team explained FallGuard's skeletal masking architecture — that no identifiable video was ever recorded, displayed, or stored, and that monitoring staff saw only a cyan wireframe overlay.

After Mrs. Sharma's safe discharge, Meera wrote to the hospital trust: "I came in worried about cameras. I left grateful for them. The fact that it protected her dignity while keeping her safe — that combination is exactly what we needed."

Clinical Outcome

Secondary fall prevented. Recovery timeline intact. Discharged on schedule with full mobility. No corrective procedure required.
